## Overview
Zhuoyan Sun's working pattern is best described as an **analytical problem-framer**: she is energised by taking an unclear business or clinical question, deciding what to measure, and carrying the work through to a recommendation someone acts on. She moves quickly across domains (structural biology to consumer-tech marketing to e-commerce) and is comfortable reporting inconvenient results.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Analytical rigour | High | Defines the metric and the test before concluding; dissertation reports mostly null effects with confidence bounds rather than overclaiming |
| Ownership | High | Prefers end-to-end responsibility (benchmark, pipeline, evaluation, report) over a slice of a larger system |
| Curiosity / learning speed | High | Picked up Tmall marketing platforms, medical rubric design and e-commerce funnels within weeks of each other |
| Stakeholder orientation | Medium-high | Repeatedly frames success as "the client or clinician could use it"; has presented to senior scientists, client stakeholders and pharma teams |
| Need for structure | Medium | Works well with ambiguity but builds her own structure fast (coding frameworks, data dictionaries, rubrics) |
| Assertiveness | Medium | Raises "fresh perspective" questions with managers (documented in onboarding notes) but is early-career and still calibrating when to push back |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Turning ambiguity into a testable plan:** converted "can our knowledge improve AI answers?" into a 246-question benchmark, rubric and A/B design.
- **Honest measurement:** flagged export defects rather than analysing bad data; validated clusters against a null; declined to claim uplift before the A/B ran.
- **Cross-functional translation:** bilingual, has bridged clinicians, marketers, engineers and client executives; writes reports that non-technical stakeholders use.
- **Persistence on unglamorous data work:** repeatedly cites pipeline optimisation (60% runtime cut at Westlake, 60% decoding-accuracy gain at USTC).

## How You Work Best
- Small teams with a clear problem owner and fast feedback (startups, client-facing teams, product analytics squads).
- Autonomy over method with regular check-ins on direction; weekly stakeholder contact rather than a ticket queue.
- Work that ends in a decision or a shipped artefact, not a slide that nobody reads.

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Production engineering depth:** has built pipelines and a Streamlit app but not owned a production service. Frame as "strong on evaluation and reliability, actively building deployment skills"; never claim production ownership.
- **Concision with non-technical audiences:** tends to explain method detail first. Frame as thoroughness; practise leading with the decision.
- **Seniority of stories:** achievements are project-scale. Frame as ownership and speed, not scale.

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

## Management Style Preferences
- Works best under a manager who sets the outcome and lets her design the method, and who gives direct, specific feedback.
- Struggles most with vague or shifting scope without a decision-maker to consult; mitigates by writing the scope down and confirming it.
